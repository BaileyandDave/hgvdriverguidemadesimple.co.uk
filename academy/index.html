Why Daily Vehicle Checks Matter

Your first job before moving an HGV is to satisfy yourself that it is safe to use.

Know that it is safe.

It's more than a routine

A walkaround gives you the opportunity to find problems before they develop into a breakdown, an incident or a roadside prohibition. Since the vehicle was last used, damage or defects may have appeared.

As HGV Driving Explained points out, these can include damaged mirrors, tyre damage, unsecured trailer doors, yard damage and connection problems.

What should your check cover?

Later lessons will explore the inspection itself. The book groups important areas into:

  • Tyres and wheels
  • Lights, reflectors, mirrors and glass
  • Bodywork and visible damage
  • Trailer, coupling and connections, where applicable
  • Cab equipment, instruments and warning indications
  • Recording and reporting defects

If you discover a defect

Don't assume somebody else has already dealt with it. Report defects through the operator's defect-reporting system, including those discovered during a journey. A defect affecting safety or legality must not be ignored; follow company procedures and obtain appropriate guidance before using an unsafe vehicle.

Never let the clock decide

The delivery may be late and the transport office may be waiting. Neither is a reason to skip safety checks. A professional driver takes the time needed and asks when unsure.

Remember: This learning resource supports, but does not replace, official guidance, formal training, the operator's safe systems of work or manufacturer instructions.
